How it works

Step by step

  1. Search for a product, scan its barcode, or type the number.
  2. We retrieve the available product data from Open Food Facts.
  3. The nutrition values are normalized (per 100 g or 100 ml).
  4. The appropriate product category is determined.
  5. The relevant Nutri-Score algorithm is applied.
  6. The grade and its components are shown.

Nutri-Score is a nutritional profiling system. It is not a complete measure of whether a food is healthy in every dietary context.

Methodology and limitations

Algorithm: the updated (2023) Nutri-Score algorithm. The older algorithm is not mixed in.

Category supported: general foods only. Beverages, fats/oils/nuts/seeds, cheese and meat have their own official rules, which this project does not implement. For those, the site shows "Score unavailable" instead of guessing.

Reference basis: all values are per 100 g. Energy is in kJ (converted from kcal only when kJ is missing).

Unfavourable points (N): energy (0 to 10), sugars (0 to 15), saturated fat (0 to 10), salt (0 to 20).

Favourable points (P): fibre (0 to 5), fruit, vegetables and legumes (0 to 5), protein (0 to 7). Protein is only counted when N is below 11.

Final score = N − P. Grades: A is −1 or lower, B is 0 to 2, C is 3 to 10, D is 11 to 18, E is 19 or more.

Missing data: if any required value is missing, no grade is calculated and the reason is shown. Missing values are never replaced with zero.

Known limitations: data is crowd-sourced and can be incomplete or wrong; the fruit, vegetable and legume percentage is often an estimate made by Open Food Facts from the ingredient list; Nutri-Score describes nutritional profile only, not whether a food suits every diet. Grades from this site can differ from the grade stored by Open Food Facts.

Tests: the scoring code is covered by automated tests of every table boundary, the protein rule, the grade boundaries and the unavailable cases.

Official sources: Santé publique France, scientific committee update report, Nature Food (2024).

Data sources

Product data comes from Open Food Facts, a collaborative database published under the ODbL licence. The grade listed by Open Food Facts is source data. Any grade labelled "calculated" is produced by this project's own scoring code.

Barcode scanning uses the open-source html5-qrcode library, which runs in your browser. Camera images are not uploaded anywhere.
